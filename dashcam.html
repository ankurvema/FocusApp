Privacy Policy

Effective 16 October 2026 Applies to DashCam for iOS

DashCam records to your phone, not to a server. Your video, your audio and your GPS traces stay in the app's own storage on the device. There are two exceptions. Both are off until you switch them on, and in both cases the data is stored in Apple's iCloud in a form we cannot read.

Video, audio and photos

On your phone

When parked Sentry person detection is enabled, camera frames are analyzed on your device to detect whether a person is present. This does not identify people. A confirmed detection starts a 30-second video saved in the app’s local gallery. Recognition results are discarded; parking event times and footage stay on your phone.

Recordings are written to DashCam's private storage area on your device. They are not uploaded, not shared and not visible to us. Loop recording deletes the oldest unlocked clip once your chosen storage cap is reached, and unlocked media is removed automatically after 30 days. Clips you lock, including those locked automatically by impact detection, are never deleted by either rule.

Exporting a clip or a time-lapse to your photo library, or sharing one through the iOS share sheet, happens only when you ask for it, and the destination is whichever app or person you choose.

Location and speed

On your phone

DashCam uses your location to show your speed, to draw the on-screen overlays and mini-map, and to record a speed and position track alongside each clip so an exported video can show what the display showed at that moment. All of it is stored on the device with the recordings.

Share Location sends your current position, and optionally the address, time and speed, to a person you pick, at the moment you tap it. Nothing is sent otherwise, and we never receive it.

Live Location is the one way your position is sent continuously, and only while you have turned it on. It is described in its own section below.

Motion and impact detection

On your phone

The accelerometer is read continuously while recording so a sudden impact can lock the surrounding footage before loop recording overwrites it. The readings are processed on the device and are not stored beyond a short rolling buffer, unless an impact occurs and you have turned on iCloud backup.

Driver monitoring

On your phone

Driver monitoring, when you enable it, watches the front camera for signs of a microsleep. Nothing is recorded while it runs. No image, no face landmark and no measurement derived from your face is stored or transmitted, to us or to anyone else.

iCloud impact backup

Your iCloud

This feature is off until you turn it on in Settings. When it is on and DashCam detects an impact, it saves a short record of that impact to your own private iCloud database, so that evidence of a crash survives even if the phone is damaged or lost. The record contains:

  • the date and time of the impact
  • the peak force measured, in g
  • where it happened, and your speed and position over the twenty seconds leading up to it
  • your device model and the app version
  • a single still frame from the camera at the moment of impact

Shortly afterwards, once that stretch of footage has finished recording, DashCam also saves a twenty-second video of the impact — the ten seconds before it and the ten seconds after — to the same private database. It is a reduced-size copy made for upload; the full-resolution original stays on your phone and is never sent. Nothing else you record is uploaded.

The record goes to the private database of your iCloud account, the same place your personal app data lives. The sensitive fields and the still image are encrypted, and Apple's design means the developer of an app cannot read the private data of its users. We cannot see your impact records or the video that goes with them. They count against your iCloud storage allowance, not ours.

Turning the setting off stops any further uploads, including a video that has been prepared but not yet sent. To remove records already saved, you can delete DashCam's data from your iCloud account in iOS Settings under your name, then iCloud.

Live Location

iCloud, encrypted

Live Location lets family or friends see each other on a map while driving. It is off until you start or join a group, and your own position is only sent while you have chosen to share it, for the time you pick (one, four or eight hours). Joining a group to see others does not share your location.

Sharing in the background is your choice. If you allow location access “Always”, your group keeps seeing you while DashCam is in the background or your phone is locked. If you do not, sharing pauses whenever DashCam is not on screen, and your group sees where you were last seen. DashCam turns background location on only while you are sharing and off again when you stop, and iOS shows its location indicator while it is on. Closing DashCam from the app switcher ends sharing either way.

While you share, DashCam sends your position every few seconds, while the app is open and, if you allowed “Always”, while it is in the background: your latitude and longitude, how accurate they are, your speed and direction of travel, the time, and the name you chose to show the group. Before it leaves your phone, this is encrypted with a key derived from your group code. It is stored in DashCam's shared iCloud database, where only someone holding the code can decrypt it. We cannot read it, and neither can Apple. What is stored readably is only a scrambled group identifier and the time the entry expires.

You can give someone the code as text, a link or a QR code. The camera is used only to read a QR code when you tap Scan QR code: nothing it sees is saved or sent, and it is not offered while a recording is running.

Anyone you give the code to can see your position while you share, and can pass the code on, so send it only to people you want to see you. Your entry is deleted when you stop sharing or leave the group, and in any case expires fifteen minutes after its last update, so a phone that is switched off or loses signal stops showing as a live position.

Usage analytics

Our analytics provider

To understand which features are used and where the app fails, DashCam sends anonymous product-usage events to Amplitude, our analytics provider. These are counts, durations, settings values and feature names, for example that a recording started, that a setting was changed, or that a purchase completed.

During setup, you may optionally share a broad vehicle type (such as RV, truck, or sedan) and occupation category. These answers are sent to Amplitude as usage profile properties so we can understand users’ requirements and prioritize improvements. You can skip the step or choose “Prefer not to say” for either answer. We do not ask for your employer, income, vehicle registration, or any free-text occupation details.

We never send coordinates, addresses, file names, video, photos, audio, or anything derived from your footage or your face. Amplitude assigns a random identifier to the installation and derives an approximate region from the network address, as described in its own documentation. There is no advertising identifier, no advertising, and no cross-app or cross-site tracking. DashCam does not sell data, and does not share it with data brokers.

Purchases

Apple

In-app purchases and DashCam Pro subscriptions are handled entirely by Apple through the App Store. We never see your payment details or your Apple Account. The app learns only whether Pro is active, and whether it comes from a subscription or from the earlier Early Supporter purchase, so it can unlock what you paid for. That status, and whether a purchase, free trial or restore completed or failed, is part of the anonymous usage analytics described above.

Diagnostic logs

On your phone

DashCam keeps a technical log on the device to help diagnose problems such as a recording that stopped or a device that overheated. Logs are kept for seven days and then deleted. They are readable in the app, and they leave the device only if you export and send them to us yourself.

Children

DashCam is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has provided us information, contact us and we will delete it.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a way that affects what leaves your device, we will update the effective date above and describe the change in the app's release notes. Continuing to use DashCam after a change means you accept the revised policy.
